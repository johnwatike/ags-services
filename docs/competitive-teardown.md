# Competitive teardown — land seismic services

Reviewed September 2026, against ag-services.org.

## The field

Land seismic splits into three tiers, and AGS competes in the third. Its website should not try to
look like the first.

**Tier 1 — integrated majors.** Viridien (the renamed CGG), SLB, TGS. They position as Earth-data and
technology companies rather than crews. Viridien opens on video of the Earth with "See the World from
a New Perspective", and navigates by market — Energy, HPC, Marine Logistics, Renewables, Defence,
Infrastructure Monitoring, Mining, Carbon Storage — rather than by service. Dark ground, white type,
video everywhere, investor content beside client content.

**Tier 2 — specialist contractors with scale.** Shearwater GeoServices, ARGAS, BGP, Dawson. Shearwater
runs a black theme, "MAKE WAVES" over textured rock, and leads with environmental metrics — 188.5
tonnes of marine debris collected, 40% reduction in offshore CO2e intensity — then four solution cards
and a six-point "Why Shearwater". ARGAS uses warm ochre and brown earth tones, a full-width video hero,
"Unlocking Earth's Natural Resources", and leans on heritage: founded 1966, the world's oldest
geoscience data acquisition company, with black-and-white archive photography.

**Tier 3 — regional land contractors.** Where AGS sits: Omani-owned, founded 2011, operating across
Oman, Tanzania, Egypt and the UK with registered offices in Dubai, Uganda, Kurdistan and Türkiye.

## What they all do identically

Dark background, white type, a video hero, a tagline about seeing or unlocking the subsurface, service
cards, an impact block, news. Two openings follow from that sameness.

**One: nobody uses white.** Every serious competitor is dark. A white-grounded site with a single
green accent reads as different before a word is read, and it reads as institutional rather than
cinematic — which is the right note for a contractor selling reliability and a zero-harm record.

**Two: they all show the surface.** Trucks, boats, crews, landscapes. None of them shows the thing the
client is actually buying, which is an image of the subsurface.

## Where AGS was weak

- "Integrated geophysical solutions across geographical boundaries" is accurate and entirely generic.
  Every competitor could use it.
- Equipment appeared as model numbers with no explanation of why a client should care which one.
- The strongest differentiators were buried: a wholly owned vibroseis fleet and drilling rigs,
  in-field processing that travels with the crew, and uphole/LVL work done in-house.
- The photography — which is genuinely good, and genuinely theirs — sat in a gallery page nobody
  reaches instead of carrying the site.
- Nothing on the site proved technical depth. There was no artefact only a geophysics company could
  have made.

## Where AGS is genuinely strong

- **Owned equipment, not rented.** Tier 3 competitors routinely sub-contract vibrators and rigs.
- **Full chain in one company.** Design, acquisition, in-field QC and office processing — what Tier 1
  offers, at Tier 3 cost.
- **Terrain range.** Desert, dense forest, farmland, populated villages, mountain and shallow water,
  with nodal spreads for the places a cable spread cannot go.
- **Local.** Omani-owned, recruiting and developing national crews in each country — a
  licence-to-operate argument in East Africa that Tier 1 cannot make as credibly.

## The positioning this redesign takes

**Show the subsurface as well as the surface.** Their own photography carries the pages; a live
wavefield runs over the hero image, the technology page shows a migrated volume beside the equipment
that produced it, the services page carries a working synthetic seismogram, and the home page has a
scroll-driven survey build that lays receiver lines, source lines and fold. None of the competitors
has anything like these.

**White and green, against a field of black.** The palette does the differentiating before the copy
starts, and the green carries the zero-harm and local-crew arguments without a sustainability page.

**Terrain as the organising idea.** The Operations page scrubs through six real terrains as you
scroll, because terrain — not channel count — is what decides how an AGS job runs.

**Turn the equipment list into an argument.** Every fleet item states what it is *for*: nodal for
permitting and safety in populated ground, in-house drilling for the uphole programme that feeds the
statics model, repeatable vibroseis sweeps for 4D.

**Do not imitate Tier 1.** No market-vertical navigation, no investor section, no digital
transformation language. The site speaks to an exploration manager choosing a crew.
